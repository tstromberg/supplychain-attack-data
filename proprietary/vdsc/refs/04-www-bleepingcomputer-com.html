VSDC Site Hacked Again to Spread Password Stealing Malware

By
Sergiu Gatlan
  • April 11, 2019
  • 08:55 AM
  • 2

The website of the free multimedia editor VSDC was breached again by hackers, this time the download links being used to distribute a banking trojan and an info stealer.

According to the Doctor Web researchers who discovered the intrusion, the hackers "hijacked download links on the website causing visitors to download a dangerous banking trojan, Win32.Bolik.2, and the Trojan.PWS.Stealer (KPOT stealer) along with the editing software."

The fact that VSDC's website has around 1,3 million every month makes this incident even more serious considering the number of potential victims.

To make things even worse, this is not the first time VSDC's website was breached, with the Chinese security firm Qihoo 360 Total Security discovering in July 2018 that malicious actors hacked their way in into the administrative side of the site on June 18, July 2, and July 6.

During that incident, the adversaries also switched the legit download links with links that redirected visitors to JavaScript files that dropped the "AZORult Stealer, X-Key Keylogger and the DarkVNC backdoor" on the victims' computers as reported by Doctor Web's malware analyst Ivan Korolev at the time.

While the VSDC team reported that the vulnerability which allowed the hackers to breach their website was patched, Doctor Web's researchers discovered that it happened again. Several times.

VSDC site hacked again. This time incident lasted for a month and distributed Win32.Bolik banker trojan that has file infector capabilities. During the last day Bolik was repalced with KPOT stealer.
Report: https://t.co/l11WGvJGrY
IoCs: https://t.co/fN00LKEFDR

— Ivan Korolev (@fe7ch) April 11, 2019

As detailed by the Doctor Web research team:

According to our researchers, the VSDC developer’s computer has been compromised several times since the previous incident. One such hack led to the website being compromised again between 2019-21-02 and 2019-23-03. This time hackers took a different approach to spreading the malware: they embedded a malicious JavaScript code inside the VSDC website. Its task was to determine the visitor’s geolocation and replace download links for users from the UK, USA, Canada and Australia. Native website links were substituted by links to another compromised website:

  • https://thedoctorwithin[.]com/video_editor_x64.exe
  • https://thedoctorwithin[.]com/video_editor_x32.exe
  • https://thedoctorwithin[.]com/video_converter.exe

Visitors who downloaded and launched the compromised VSDC video editor and video converter have had their computers potentially infected with a multicomponent polymorphic banking Trojan tagged by Doctor Web as Win32.Bolik.2 and with a variant of the KPOT Stealer Trojan.

The banking Trojan is designed to "perform web injections, traffic intercepts, key-logging and stealing information from different bank-client systems" and the KPOT info stealer will swipe "information from browsers, Microsoft accounts, several messengers and some other programs."

As disclosed by the Doctor Web team, at least 565 users had their computers tainted with the Win32.Bolik.2 banking Trojan, while another 83 downloaded VSDC software infected with KPOT Stealer.

"The VSDC developers were notified about the threat; and at the present moment, download links were restored to the originals," says Doctor Web.

BleepingComputer has reached out to the VSDC team for more details but did not receive an answer prior to publication.

Update April 11 2019 16:21 EDT: A VSDC spokesperson got back to us with the following statement:

In the past few months, the popularity of our video editing software has increased, and as our technical team reports, so has the activity of malicious actors willing to take advantage of our website traffic. 

Even with a fully-functioning security system guarding our website, we can confirm that it was shortly affected by the attack during the indicated period of time, and unlike the previous case mentioned in your article, the hackers had taken a new approach. 

The administrative side of the website and the program files were not affected. Meanwhile, the possible  security vulnerability was detected and patched.

This time we have resorted to an innovative protection algorithm to prevent the attacks of similar nature in the future, yet, amid safety concerns, we aren't able to disclose the details. 
        
At this moment, all of our website visitors and software users have no reasons to be worried.
